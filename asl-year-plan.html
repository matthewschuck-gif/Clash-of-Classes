The ASL Summit Trail

A year-long introduction to American Sign Language for one middle school student. There are 36 weekly sessions in nine monthly climbs, and each month lines up with that month's Mountaineer Mindset trait.

36 sessions · 1 per week 20–30 minutes each ~350 signs by the Summit 9 Peak Checks + Summit Showcase

Every session

The Climb routine

Run every session the same way. Your student will know what's coming, and you'll barely need to plan. The times add up to 25 minutes.

3 MIN

Trailhead

Fingerspelling warm-up. Spell 5 words and have them read each one back, then switch roles.

5 MIN

Switchbacks

Review last week's signs. Sign them in a random order; the student says or signs back the meaning.

8 MIN

New Ground

Teach this week's 8–12 signs. Show each one, have them copy it, then use it in a short sentence.

7 MIN

The Climb

The game or activity for the week. Use the signs, not just the vocabulary list.

2 MIN

Trail Journal

Exit check: the student signs 3 things they learned today. Check off the week.

Only 20 minutes? Cut the Climb to 3 minutes and fold Switchbacks into the Trailhead. Have 30? Add a "Culture Corner" chat or a second round of the game.

Games & resources

Go-to games for two people

  • Mirror Me: you sign and they copy, then switch. Good for handshapes.
  • Spell It Back: fingerspell a word and they write or say it. Speed up each round.
  • Sign Detective: watch a short Deaf-made video and count the signs they recognize.
  • Beat the Timer: how many review signs can they produce in 60 seconds?
  • Silent 20 Questions: guess a person or thing with yes/no signs only.
  • Draw What I Sign: you sign a scene and they sketch it. Good for classifiers.

Check every sign here

  • Lifeprint / ASL University: free lessons and a video dictionary by Dr. Bill Vicars
  • Handspeak: dictionary plus Deaf culture articles
  • SignASL: several signers per word, so you can see variations
  • The ASL App (free, made by Deaf creators): good for practice at home

Teaching tips

  • Watch a video of each new sign before the session. Signs vary by region, so pick one version and stick with it.
  • Your student picks a dominant hand on day 1 and keeps it all year.
  • Sign with voice off whenever you can. Gestures and pointing count.
  • Words in CAPS (like THANK-YOU) are glosses, the standard way to write signs.
  • If you can, invite a Deaf guest or watch Deaf storytellers. It's the best way to see ASL at its best.
